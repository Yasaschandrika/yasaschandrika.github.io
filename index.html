SENSING • ENERGY • LTSPICE

Piezoelectric Smart Road System

Energy Harvesting & Traffic Density Detection

A smart-road concept exploring how vehicle pressure can be used for both energy harvesting and traffic-density sensing. I designed and simulated the energy-harvesting circuit in LTspice, while the traffic-density and adaptive signal-control portion remains a proposed system design.

Energy Harvesting

Piezoelectric-source model → bridge rectifier → 4700 µF storage capacitor.

Simulation

200 ms transient analysis with a 1 kΩ load.

Observed Output

The storage-node voltage rises with periodic ripple and reaches approximately 70–72 V at its highest point in the shown run.

Traffic Concept

Pressure pulses → microcontroller counting → density estimate → adaptive signal timing.

LTspice transient simulation — storage-node waveform over 200 ms.

Download LTspice file ↗
DESIGNED

Rectifier and capacitor energy-storage circuit.

SIMULATED

Transient response in LTspice using modeled piezoelectric outputs.

PROPOSED

Vehicle-density detection and dynamic traffic-signal control.

Simulation note: the LTspice inputs are sinusoidal voltage-source models representing piezoelectric outputs. This is a circuit-level simulation, not a measurement from a physical road prototype.

DNA × DATA × ELECTRONICS

DNA Data Storage

Can a molecular storage medium become an electronic system?

I’m exploring DNA-based data storage because it sits at an unusual intersection: information theory, sensing, electronics, molecular systems and computing. The attraction is extraordinary storage density and long-term stability; the engineering challenge is turning that molecular process into a practical, addressable and reliable storage system.

THE ECE ANGLE

How do we sense, address, read and write information when the storage medium is molecular rather than electronic?

THE HARD PROBLEMS

Read/write speed, synthesis and sequencing cost, random access, signal detection and error correction.

WHAT I’M FOLLOWING

Electronic and electrochemical interfaces, molecular sensing, encoding/decoding and scalable architectures.
